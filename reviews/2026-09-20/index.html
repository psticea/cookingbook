Paul's Cookbook
UI & content review

The food photography, warm colors and compact, ad-free presentation already give this site a welcoming identity. The biggest opportunities are not a new visual style: they are a better desktop layout, a reliable cooking workflow, clearer recipe discovery, and more trustworthy supporting information.

Prepared for Paul's Cookbook after running the existing React/Vite application locally and exercising its actual interfaces.

No application code changed. This folder contains only the review and screenshots. Findings distinguish directly observed behavior, source-backed evidence, and proposed enhancements.

Coverage: home, search, filtering, sorting controls, recipe ingredients/instructions/nutrition, serving adjustments, preferences, Cooking Basics, Ingredient Prices, About, and invalid URLs. Romanian and English; light and dark themes; normal and large text; desktop, tablet and small-phone widths.

Overall verdict

A strong visual foundation with fixable workflow and content-trust problems.

This feels like a real person's practical collection, not a content-farm recipe site. That is a competitive advantage. Recipes are reached without an oversized marketing introduction, photos do useful work, and language, theme and text-size preferences make the collection feel considered.

The experience is currently stronger as a mobile browsing catalogue than as a desktop cookbook or an active cooking companion. On a wide screen the catalogue is squeezed into a phone-like strip. During cooking, the interface asks people to move between ingredients and instructions, then forgets their ingredient checks. Prices look precise despite calculation gaps. Several small details make the interface feel less finished than the imagery deserves.

What is worth preserving

  • The visual identity: warm off-white surfaces, food-led photography, olive accents and expressive serif headings. Improve hierarchy within this world; do not replace it with a generic dashboard.
  • The low-friction premise: no account wall or lengthy life story before the ingredients. A small personal collection does not need a marketing landing page.
  • The useful foundations: bilingual content, adjustable servings, ingredient checkboxes, a recoverable empty-search state, and preferences that survived a reload in the browser.
  • The author's voice: specific shopping and preparation notes are more valuable than generic descriptions. Make these a consistent editorial feature.

What to fix first

Priority reflects user impact, not how visually dramatic a change would be. P1 means a core journey or trust issue; P2 means a meaningful improvement; P3 means refinement. No launch-wide P0 outage was observed.

Detailed UI findings

P1 · Observed + source-backed

Remove the abrupt desktop width constraint

The shared application wrapper uses lg:max-w-[40vw]. This is not just generous whitespace: it makes the content substantially smaller precisely when the viewport becomes larger. The card grid switches to three columns inside that reduced space.

Recommended change
Use a fluid container with fixed comfortable gutters and a sensible maximum width. Give the catalogue approximately 1100-1200px on large screens, subject to visual validation. Make columns respond to available card width, not viewport alone. Give recipe pages their own reading layout: a compact ingredient column beside a wider method column on desktop, with roughly 60-75 characters per line for prose.
Why this matters
People can compare meals without decoding small thumbnails and tightly wrapped names. Desktop becomes genuinely better for planning, rather than a magnified margin around the mobile site.
Acceptance criteria
No abrupt width collapse around 1024px; card titles remain readable; layout is intentional at 768, 1024, 1280 and 1440px. Do not simply stretch prose across the full catalogue width.

P1 · Reproduced

Do not erase progress when the cook reads the next step

On Marry Me Chicken Pasta, checking the first ingredient, opening Instructions, and returning to Ingredients unchecks that ingredient. Reloading also clears it. Switching tabs is a routine cooking action, not a request to reset the recipe.

Recommended change
Keep recipe interaction state above the changing panels. Preserve checks while switching tabs and adjusting servings. Provide an explicit, named reset action. Session persistence across reloads is useful, but decide whether returning on another day should restore the old cooking session rather than silently making that decision.
Improve the wider workflow
On desktop, show ingredients and method together. On mobile, keep a compact, sticky recipe navigation strip and remember each panel's scroll position. Repeat relevant quantities in steps where helpful. Make nutrition secondary during active cooking, not an equal competing task.
Acceptance criteria
Check three ingredients, read instructions, return, change servings, and rotate the device: the same three checks remain. A separate reset clears them predictably. No checklist state leaks between recipes.

A related missing baseline: print-media inspection with Ingredients selected did not include the method. A printable recipe should contain the title, yield, all ingredients, all steps and author/source notes, irrespective of the currently selected tab.

Serving adjustments must also agree with the method

A second cooking correctness issue was reproduced on Smashed Beef Burger: increasing two servings to three changes the ingredient list from 200g to 300g beef, but the instructions still say "Divide the 200 g of beef into two 100 g balls." This is more important than rounding fractional onions. Model quantity references explicitly or rewrite steps so they remain valid at a different yield. Do not blindly multiply oven temperatures, pan sizes or cooking durations.

Additional data examples: burgers-and-wraps/smashed-beef-burger.json:173,183; fixed soup water/noodle amounts in soups-and-stews/chicken-soup.json:171-175; fixed rice quantity in soups-and-stews/chicken-taco-bowls.json:220. Paths are relative to src/data/recipes.

Reproduction: /recipe/marry-me-chicken-pasta. Root cause: local checked state in src/components/IngredientList.tsx:24-25 is discarded by conditional mounting in src/pages/RecipePage.tsx:301-319. The printable outcome was inspected in emulated print media, not on physical paper.

P1 · Reproduced

The closed drawer should not be a second invisible interface

The menu is visually translated offscreen but remains a dialog in the accessibility tree. With the drawer closed, pressing Tab after the visible Menu button focuses the offscreen Close button, then the chicken, beef and fish filters. At desktop width, their bounding boxes begin beyond the viewport.

This is a practical keyboard problem, not merely a missing annotation. A visitor can lose track of focus without having opened the menu. With the drawer open, Shift+Tab from Close did not remain contained within the dialog; Escape did not reliably restore focus to its trigger in the observed sequence.

Recommended change
Remove closed menu content from interaction and the accessibility tree. When open, manage initial focus, contain focus, make the background inert, close on Escape and return focus to the trigger. Keep the trigger's expanded state accurate. Use a proven accessible dialog pattern rather than a visual transform alone.
Other keyboard work
The recipe tablist did not respond to ArrowRight. Implement the full tab keyboard pattern, including appropriate focus order and panel relationships, or use simpler section links. Localize the serving controls: Romanian pages currently announce English "Increase servings" and "Decrease servings."
Acceptance criteria
Complete search, open a recipe, adjust servings and read each section with the keyboard alone. Closed-menu controls must never receive focus. Screen-reader testing should follow; this review did not substitute a full assistive-technology audit.

Sources: src/components/SideMenu.tsx:22-39,45-80; src/components/Header.tsx:64-68; src/pages/RecipePage.tsx:235-319.

P1 · Reproduced

Search should understand the way people actually type

In Romanian, rosii returned no recipes, while roșii returned two. The search term pui returned eight title matches, whereas the chicken filter produced nine results. Users are not told that text search and ingredient filtering have different coverage.

After searching, opening a recipe, and following Home, the query was empty and all 33 recipes returned. That makes comparing two possible dinners needlessly repetitive.

Recommended change
Normalize case and diacritics. Index titles plus useful ingredient names and validated aliases, with title matches ranked first. Make language behavior explicit; matching common English/Romanian ingredient equivalents would be helpful, but only add translations you can maintain. Keep query, category, sort and filters in URL state so Back and copied links preserve intent.
Helpful copy
Use a descriptive result count, such as "8 recipes", rather than an isolated "8". When a search has no results, distinguish "Clear search" from "Reset filters". Keep the current friendly empty-state tone; its recovery button successfully cleared the tested query.
Acceptance criteria
rosii and roșii return the same relevant recipes. A recipe containing chicken can be found by chicken even if its title omits it. Back restores query, result set and browsing position.

Sources: src/pages/HomePage.tsx:28-89,98-115. Search currently checks only the selected language's title, starts at two characters, and does not trim surrounding whitespace.

P1 · Measured

Make large text and dark mode useful all the way down

At 390px, the English Frittata page with large text remained within the viewport. At 320px, the same page expanded to a 387px document. Ingredient quantities, ingredient names and right-aligned prices compete for horizontal space. The three-part time/serving/price row also becomes cramped.

Several supporting labels stay tiny despite the large-text setting. Recipe metadata was measured at 10px. In settled dark mode, these labels used rgb(108,109,118) over rgb(29,29,34), a contrast ratio of approximately 3.27:1. That is below the 4.5:1 target for normal-sized text. The 11px Personal Notes heading measured approximately 4.01:1 on the same surface.

The serving buttons are 28 × 28px at normal root size. Aim for at least 44 × 44px for forgiving interaction while cooking. This is an ergonomic recommendation, not a claim that every smaller target automatically fails WCAG.

Recommended change
Let ingredient names take the available width; move unit price below the ingredient or hide detailed costs behind an optional cost breakdown. Stack metadata when required. Increase important secondary text, reduce all-caps letter spacing on dense labels, and derive muted text colors from tested contrast tokens in both themes.
Acceptance criteria
No horizontal page scrolling at a 320px CSS viewport with large text. No loss of information at 200% text enlargement. Labels remain readable without relying on color alone; normal text reaches 4.5:1 and large text reaches 3:1 against its actual background.

Sources: src/pages/RecipePage.tsx:190,195-226; src/components/IngredientList.tsx:102-137; src/components/PersonalNotes.tsx:22-23. Metadata and notes backgrounds are solid, so no image blending is involved in the stated contrast ratios.

P2 · Observed

Give discovery its own place; let settings be settings

The menu combines 20 keyword filters, eight recipe categories, three secondary links and language/theme/text preferences. It is the place to change how the website looks and the place to decide what to eat. These are different tasks.

The catalogue also starts with a bare count, a Categories switch and Name/Time/Price sorting controls. The switch means "group the results", while the menu's Categories section means "jump to a category section", not filter the results. That distinction is not obvious from the repeated label. Source review also shows that grouping off removes those jump targets, while the category controls remain available.

All selected keywords use AND matching, even within a group. Choosing chicken and beef therefore means a recipe must have both, rather than either. Explain that behavior or use OR within groups where it matches the intended browsing model. The ingredient list is also limited to its first 12 filter entries without a way to reveal the rest.

Recommended change
Place a clearly labeled Filters control beside search, display active filter chips above results and keep preferences in the menu. On desktop, use a compact filter rail or popover without sacrificing card width. On mobile, show an explicit result-count action such as "Show 9 recipes" within the filter sheet. Label the switch "Group by category".
Better entry points
With 33 recipes, start with a few useful, curated shortcuts: quick meals, chicken, vegetarian and cooking basics. Do not show every tag at once. A time shortcut must use reliable total-time metadata, and dietary collections must be manually validated.
Acceptance criteria
A first-time visitor can find a quick chicken recipe without opening site preferences. Selected filters and their effect are visible outside the drawer. Clear the last filter in one action.

Sources: src/pages/HomePage.tsx:98-104,153-160,228-306; src/components/FiltersSection.tsx:29-33,64-69.

P2 · Observed / recommendation

Let each recipe card answer: what, how long, and why this one?

Photos and titles already make the cards appealing. But the image overlays show values like "260m" and a money-bag icon followed by "6.22". A visitor must infer the currency, whether that price is per serving, and whether time includes waiting.

Use readable total time, such as "4 hr 20 min", and a qualified cost such as "~6.20 RON / serving" only when the estimate is complete enough to justify it. Put secondary metadata on a quiet, consistent line rather than asking badges to carry unexplained numbers. Keep title wrapping natural instead of shrinking type.

A short, concrete distinction is more valuable than another decorative badge: "One pan", "Good for leftovers", or "My weeknight version" when the recipe genuinely supports it. At this catalogue size, manual curation is more valuable than a complex recommendation engine.

P3 polish: use singular forms for "1 recipe" / "1 rețetă"; standardize punctuation and title capitalization; make count and metadata labels read as language rather than database fields.

P2 · Reproduced

Finish the quiet corners that affect trust

About: the page's Comments heading is followed by no working comments interface. The browser reports that https://giscus.app/client.js is blocked by the document's script policy; no comments iframe is created. Source review also found placeholder repository/category identifiers. Fixing CSP alone will not configure the integration. Either configure it completely with narrowly scoped resource permissions or remove the empty promise; do not broadly weaken the policy. Show a useful loading/error/fallback state if comments remain.

Invalid URLs: an invalid recipe ID initially gets a helpful "Recipe not found" message, but automatically redirects home after three seconds. That removes the user's opportunity to read and act at their own pace. An unknown top-level route such as /cookingbook/not-a-page renders a blank body. Use persistent, helpful recovery pages with explicit navigation instead.

Ingredient Prices: the searchable 128-item reference is a good transparency feature. However, its shared menu displays recipe filters and category controls with empty callbacks. In the browser, activating Chicken left its pressed state false; selecting Pasta left the user on Prices with no effect. Wire these to a meaningful action or remove them. Also add price date, currency, geographical/store assumptions and estimation limitations. "Mass", "Volume" and "Piece" are implementation categories; people primarily need "RON/kg", "RON/litre" or "RON/item".

Cooking Basics: four tips ending "To be continued..." read as an unfinished page. Either label it honestly as a short getting-started checklist or expand it into linked, practical lessons. Connect the rice and polenta recipes to this learning route instead of making users discover a second, similarly named Basics category.

P1 · Observed + data-backed

Two decimal places should not disguise placeholder costs

The initial Romanian catalogue produced repeated pricing warnings for unsupported units: spinach measured in căni, rice in cană, some chicken quantities in grams against piece-based prices, and other ingredient/unit combinations. React development rendering repeats some messages, so the console count is not a count of unique broken ingredients.

The calculation substitutes 0.20 RON per serving for each ingredient it cannot price, independently of quantity or market value. That is an undisclosed placeholder, not an estimated price derived from the ingredient. Jasmine rice appeared at 0.20 RON per serving while the console reported an unsupported rice unit. The result is consistent with this fallback mechanism, not evidence that rice is genuinely that cheap.

Recommended change
Separate machine-readable canonical quantities and units from translated display strings. Track coverage and return an explicit partial/unavailable estimate rather than substituting an arbitrary constant. Never use a generic cup-to-gram conversion for every ingredient; density and ingredient form matter. Reconcile the ingredient breakdown with the header total in both languages.
How to present it
Use "Estimated cost" consistently. Include "excludes 2 ingredients" when incomplete, or withhold the number. Add an optional details view with missing items, price date and assumptions. Only rank recipes by price on a defined, comparable basis.
Acceptance criteria
Language changes do not change the cost of the same quantities. The sum of displayed ingredient costs reconciles with total and per-serving cost within rounding. Unsupported units produce visible qualification, not a misleading cheap recipe.

The calculator reads translated unit text, so localization currently participates in calculation. A missing required price rate can also become zero while still being marked as matched. Successful conversion is not always reliable conversion: generic spoon-to-mass and slice/packet-to-piece assumptions deserve ingredient-specific review. Calculate at full precision and round only for display rather than independently rounding and summing whole-recipe and per-serving values.

Source: src/utils/pricing.ts:38,73-124,133-185,209-216. No corpus-wide percentage of accurately priced ingredients is claimed; warnings contain repeated calculations, and matching a price ID does not prove unit/rate validity. An unchanged Frittata price between languages is not evidence of a stale cached total: both translations can reach the same fallback.

Concrete data cleanup targets

Parfaits use 900g of canned pineapple against a generic per-piece pineapple price. Chicken thigh is priced per piece but appears in recipes measured in grams. Roast chicken's lemon juice points to the whole-lemon record even though a dedicated lemon-juice record exists. Corn salad's lime juice similarly points to whole lime. These mappings need a compatible purchasing basis or a documented yield conversion.

Sources: breakfast/tropical-yogurt-parfaits.json:51-59; main-courses/roasted-chicken-and-potatoes.json:40-47; salads-and-bites/mexican-street-corn-salad.json:121-131, under src/data/recipes; src/data/prices.json:213-218,332-337,409-414,654-659. The price catalogue has no collection date, market, retailer or estimation-policy fields.

P1 · Data-backed planning issue

Time-to-serve must include the waiting that changes the plan

Tropical Yogurt Parfaits display 20 minutes, but their method requires overnight refrigeration. Butter Chicken's 215 minutes includes at least three hours of marination. Taco Bowls list 260 minutes while the method offers four-hour and eight-hour slow-cooker schedules. The same field therefore mixes different meanings across recipes.

Introduce active preparation, cooking, passive waiting and total elapsed time with a clear policy. Parfaits should communicate "20 min active + overnight chilling"; slow-cooker recipes should show the relevant alternatives. This is a content correction needed before a "quick meals" shortcut or time sorting can be trusted.

Sources under src/data/recipes: breakfast/tropical-yogurt-parfaits.json:9,91,97; soups-and-stews/butter-chicken.json:8,213; soups-and-stews/chicken-taco-bowls.json:8,218-219. The schema describes the single field as preparation time at src/schemas/recipe.schema.json:65-68.

P2 · Editorial improvement

Give every recipe the same practical contract

A reader should know what they are making, how much it yields, how long it actually takes, and what success looks like. On the current recipe header, "Prep time" labels the combined time value; this can be mistaken for chopping/preparation time rather than total cooking time. Replace that ambiguity with prep, cook and total time, plus passive waiting where it materially changes planning.

Taxonomy: Krem a la Krem Cake appears in Salads & Bites. That may satisfy the current schema but contradicts how people look for dessert. Add a coherent dessert classification before growing the sweet recipe collection. Likewise, a dish's method and its meal type should be separate facets; one category should not have to express both.

Scaling: five to six servings of Marry Me Chicken produced "1.2" onions and "4.8" garlic cloves. These are mathematically consistent but awkward in a kitchen. Keep the exact scaled quantity available and introduce sensible ingredient-aware presentation, not silent rounding of every amount.

Localization and dietary labeling

A native Romanian editing pass would make the writing more natural. Examples include "Aduce o oală..." instead of the imperative "Adu...", "la un fierbere ușor", and Frittata's literal "ouăle sunt fixate". Add practical metric equivalents for a 10-inch pan and a 5-quart slow cooker. Standardize tone, sentence casing and diacritics; correct "marunțită" to "mărunțită" and the English "tumeric" typo.

Optional ingredients should be structured data, not just prose. The marinara recipe already distinguishes optional chicken from its vegetarian base; apply that clarity consistently in filters and cost estimates. Do not treat vegetarian labels as allergen guarantees.

Reconcile taxonomy as the collection grows: seven used keywords have no filter definition, including cabbage, bacon, noodles and broccoli. Difficulty should come from one authoritative field rather than a second, inconsistently maintained keyword list.

P2 · Observed

Distinguish Paul's notes from the visitor's notes

The recipe's "Personal Notes" section displays the author's existing commentary. In the reviewed page, it is not an editable field for the visitor. The README advertises adding and saving custom recipe notes, which creates a mismatch between documented and actual experience.

Rename the existing section "Paul's notes" or "Cook's notes". Make source URLs proper, descriptive links. If visitor notes are introduced later, label them separately as "Your notes" and state their storage boundary, for example "Saved only in this browser" if that is how persistence works. An account or sync system is not necessary just to clarify ownership.

P2 now · P1 before expanding nutrition coverage

Make nutrition informative rather than over-authoritative

The nutrition panel has thoughtful structure: calories, macronutrients, daily-value rows, strengths, cautions and an estimate disclaimer. On Marry Me Chicken, it explicitly warns that saturated fat is high. Preserve those explanations.

However, a large 7.9/10 nutrition score suggests a broadly validated judgment unless its methodology is easy to understand. The same panel can say "Good" while highlighting a nutrient at 75% of a daily value. That is not necessarily a contradiction, but the weighting must be clear.

Important implementation finding: the reusable panel hardcodes the "Good" classification, the praise for protein/potassium/calcium, and the saturated-fat "75%" sentence independently of its input. Those statements fit the current single populated recipe, but would not adapt to a different recipe's nutrition. Derive commentary from the actual values or remove it before adding nutrition to more recipes. A low score must not automatically receive a "Good" label.

Prioritize estimated energy and macros; make the composite score optional or explain it directly beside the score. State reference population, calculation method, provenance and missing-data treatment. Do not imply a health endorsement from a homemade formula. Make absent nutrition distinguishable from zero, and maintain consistent coverage before promoting nutrition as a collection-wide feature.

The data audit found nutrition on only Marry Me Chicken Pasta. Its macro-energy arithmetic is internally close to the stated calories (approximately 772 vs. 770 kcal), but that does not verify ingredients, portion yield or nutrient accuracy. Record source assumptions and calculation date before expanding this feature.

Source: src/components/NutritionPanel.tsx:44-57,82-108,138-156,175-182,269-282. This is a content-trust and presentation assessment, not an independent nutritional analysis or medical review.

P2 · Observed + source review

Give recipes a useful identity outside the app

Home, Frittata, Marry Me Chicken, About and Cooking Basics retained "Cooking Book - Rețete Delicioase" as the browser title, including English views. A bookmark, shared link or browser tab should say which recipe it contains.

Add localized route-specific titles and descriptions, canonical URLs, meaningful social preview metadata and Recipe structured data populated from the existing recipe records. Do not add ratings, reviews or nutritional facts that the site does not actually have. If crawler/social-preview reliability matters, validate rendered HTML and consider prerendering recipe routes rather than assuming every consumer will execute the client app.

The homepage also had no h1. A short, useful heading can establish identity without adding a giant hero. Keep the first recipe row close to the top.

Runtime inspection of the burger page confirmed no description, canonical link, Open Graph tags or JSON-LD. The HTML also references the starter vite.svg favicon, which does not exist in this checkout. Replace it with a branded icon. A sitemap would help describe intended indexable recipes; a missing robots file alone is not an indexing failure.

Static source: index.html:5,17; runtime metadata inspection on /recipe/smashed-beef-burger. The public fallback page still uses "Recipe Website".

P2 · Editorial maintenance

Make validation protect the content promises

The existing validator successfully accepted all 33 recipes after dependencies were restored. This is useful structural validation; it does not establish cooking correctness, complete costing or consistent time semantics.

Extend checks to enforce the invariants the documentation already suggests: unique recipe IDs, filename/category correspondence, known filter vocabulary, compatible price units, image existence and duplicate JSON-key detection. Keep human recipe walk-throughs separate from schema validation.

The recipe documentation also points at an incorrect schema location, and recommends adding a $schema property that the current schema rejects through additionalProperties: false. Repair those instructions before using them as a recipe-contribution workflow. These maintenance changes are less visible than layout work, but prevent the same content defects from returning.

Sources: scripts/validate-recipes.cjs:55-59; src/data/recipes/VALIDATION.md:73,93,97,336,391; src/data/recipes/README.md:74-77,129.

Recommended design direction

Build on the personal cookbook identity with practical annotations and more usable layouts.

The site already has enough visual personality. Spend design effort making its purpose clearer rather than adding animation, a new palette, or a large promotional hero. Food, practical annotations and quiet controls should carry the identity.

Home: help people choose dinner

Keep the brand and search at the top. Add a compact one-line introduction, then useful shortcuts, an explicit filter control, a labeled result count and generous food cards. Preserve category grouping as a view option, not as the dominant mystery switch. Move language access higher if bilingual sharing is important; currently it is in the drawer or at the end of a long catalogue.

Recipes I cook at home.
A personal collection in Romanian and English, with practical notes and no ads.

Suggested copy, not a replacement made to the site. It deliberately avoids unverified claims such as "all tested", "healthy" or "ready in 30 minutes".

Recipe: separate choosing from cooking

Use the photo, title and a brief description to establish the dish. Immediately below, show total time, yield and a qualified cost. Then make ingredients and method the main event. On desktop, let them coexist. On mobile, make switching predictable and preserve progress. Keep author notes close to the relevant cooking information; place nutrition and full price breakdowns behind secondary disclosure.

Small improvements with a large perceived-quality return

  • Use "30 min", "2 hr 20 min" and "RON / serving" instead of compressed or truncated labels.
  • Use consistent, restrained icons. Food photography should provide the color; badges should explain, not decorate.
  • Raise secondary text contrast and remove unnecessary uppercase from long labels.
  • Make selected filters, active sections and keyboard focus visually distinct in both themes.
  • Use fewer borders and price labels in the cooking view; reserve the densest detail for people asking for it.

Do not prioritize yet: accounts, a meal planner, AI recommendations, elaborate transitions, a full brand redesign or more dashboard-style nutrition graphics. None addresses the strongest observed friction.

Where different visitors will struggle

A first-time Romanian visitor: types without diacritics, gets no results, and has to discover ingredient filtering inside Menu. The empty-state recovery helps, but the initial failure is avoidable.

A cook with a phone beside the stove: checks ingredients, reads instructions, returns to a reset list, and must remember what is already prepared. This is the clearest mismatch between the interface and the real activity.

A keyboard or large-text user: encounters offscreen drawer controls or horizontal overflow. Accessibility preferences are a good start, but the surrounding layout and interaction must honor them.

A budget-conscious planner: compares precise prices without knowing that some ingredient costs were replaced with fixed placeholders. Accurate uncertainty matters more here than a prettier price badge.

A practical improvement sequence

These are ordered work packages, not calendar estimates. Confirm desired behavior before implementation where the choice is product-specific, especially persistent cooking sessions and visitor notes.

First
Remove friction

Fix the shared width constraint and breakpoint behavior; preserve ingredient checks; remove the hidden drawer from keyboard navigation; fix the 320px large-text overflow; normalize search diacritics; qualify incomplete price totals.

Exit condition: browse, choose, cook and return to results without losing context, information or focus.

Next
Clarify the experience

Separate Filters from Menu; expose selected filters; refine card metadata; add an intentional desktop recipe layout and complete printing; rename author notes; correct time labels and singular/plural copy; add catch-all routing and comments failure handling.

Exit condition: a new visitor can explain every prominent control and every displayed number without guessing.

Then
Improve the collection

Run an editorial pass over the existing recipes: units, time accounting, yield, ingredient-method agreement, source attribution, taxonomy and practical author tips. Define a price and nutrition methodology. Turn Cooking Basics into either a complete short checklist or a useful linked guide.

Exit condition: every recipe follows a consistent, trustworthy cooking contract in both languages.

Finally
Improve reach

Add route-specific metadata, valid Recipe structured data and reliable share previews. Measure the production build before optimizing performance. Consider saving favorites or an optional cooking mode only after the underlying reading and state behavior are reliable.

Exit condition: shared recipes look intentional, are discoverable, and load well on the actual hosting setup.

Method & limitations

Local environment: existing React 18 / TypeScript / Vite application, served at http://127.0.0.1:5173/cookingbook/. The first start failed because Vite dependencies were absent; dependencies were restored from the existing lockfile, then the server responded with HTTP 200. No package manifest or lockfile changes were needed.

Browser review: actual page navigation and interactions in Chromium; layout inspection at 320, 390, 768, 1023, 1024, 1280 and 1440 CSS pixels. Most detailed phone interaction used 390 × 844; desktop used 1440 × 1000. This was viewport emulation, not physical-device testing.

Evidence discipline: reproduced defects are labeled as such. Design recommendations are judgments, not measured conversion improvements. Small fixed-size text contrast was calculated from settled opaque foreground/background colors. A transient contradictory selected-tab color reading was not treated as a persistent defect after a follow-up showed the correct dark-theme text.

Source assessment: two separate assessors covered interface source and recipe/data evidence, followed by targeted cross-checks against the browser walkthrough. Interface coverage included five pages, 21 top-level components, four preference/storage hooks, styling/tokens and a bounded pricing follow-up. Content coverage included all 33 recipe files and both languages, categories, filter vocabulary, translations, prices, schema, validation documentation and static metadata. Baseline commit: 9ee825f. Source line references refer to this reviewed checkout.
